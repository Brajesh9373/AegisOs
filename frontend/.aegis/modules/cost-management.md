# AegisAI Cost Management Architecture Specification

This document defines the definitive, permanent Cost Management Architecture for AegisAI. It establishes the rules, tracking mechanisms, and governance models for calculating and controlling the financial impact of AI operations.

## 1. Cost Philosophy
In an Enterprise AI OS, compute is money. Generative AI introduces highly variable, usage-based costs that can rapidly spiral out of control if left unmonitored. AegisAI believes in absolute financial transparency. The platform never hides costs; it tracks every token and every execution, attributing them directly to the human or department responsible. 

## 2. Objectives
The Cost Management Architecture must:
* Track AI Provider costs (tokens) and infrastructure costs (compute/storage) at a granular level.
* Enforce hard quotas and budget limits to prevent cost overruns.
* Provide FinOps visibility so organizations can accurately charge back departments.
* Proactively alert owners before budgets are exhausted.

## 3. Cost Architecture
The Cost Engine is a background consumer on the Event Bus. When a Task completes, the Runtime emits a `CostIncurred` event containing the raw usage metrics (e.g., prompt tokens, completion tokens, execution time). The Cost Engine enriches this event with the current pricing matrix, calculates the monetary value, and persists it to an OLAP datastore for reporting.

## 4. Cost Centers
Logical groupings for financial attribution. Every Workspace maps to a specific Cost Center, which rolls up to a Department.

## 5. Organization Cost
The aggregate sum of all expenditures across the entire tenant. Used by Finance teams for vendor reconciliation.

## 6. Department Cost
The aggregate cost incurred by all Workspaces within a Department. Evaluated against the Department's allocated budget.

## 7. User Cost
The cost generated by Tasks initiated by a specific human User.

## 8. Agent Cost
The cost generated by a specific AI persona. Helps identify inefficient or looping Agents.

## 9. Provider Cost
Costs segmented by the upstream vendor (e.g., OpenAI vs. Anthropic) to optimize procurement strategies.

## 10. Model Cost
Costs segmented by specific models (e.g., GPT-4o vs. Llama-3-70b) to track the financial impact of model selection.

## 11. Tool Cost
Costs associated with specific integrations, especially those that incur per-API-call charges (e.g., a paid data enrichment API).

## 12. Runtime Cost
The infrastructural compute cost (e.g., EC2 hours) required to run the AegisAI Task Engine, typically calculated using a standard rate card.

## 13. Storage Cost
The cost of retaining Knowledge vectors, attachments, and Memory in Object Storage.

## 14. Budget Management
Organizations and Departments are assigned monthly, quarterly, or annual budgets (in USD or an internal token currency).

## 15. Quotas
Hard limits placed on specific entities (e.g., "Agent SupportBot cannot spend more than $50/day").

## 16. Spending Policies
Guardian policies that evaluate cost risk before execution. Example: "If estimated task cost > $5.00, require Human Approval."

## 17. Cost Monitoring
A dedicated Cost Dashboard visualizes burn rates, highest-spending Agents, and anomalies in real-time.

## 18. Cost Forecasting
The Engine uses historical trailing averages to project the end-of-month spend, enabling proactive intervention.

## 19. Cost Optimization
The system can recommend optimizations (e.g., "Agent X uses GPT-4o for simple summarization; switching to GPT-4o-mini would save $500/mo").

## 20. Cost Reporting
Automated CSV/PDF reports distributed to Cost Center owners at the end of the billing cycle for chargeback processing.

## 21. Cost Alerts
Triggered at predefined thresholds (e.g., 50%, 80%, 95%, 100% of budget). Alerts route via the Notification Engine.

## 22. Cost Audit
Cost metrics are immutable once calculated and are stored alongside the operational Audit Ledger for financial compliance.

## 23. Future Expansion
The architecture supports future integration with cloud provider billing APIs (AWS Cost Explorer) to ingest and unify raw infrastructure costs with application-level AI costs.

## 24. Permanent Constraints
* The Customer inherently owns all AI provider costs (AegisAI is self-hosted).
* The platform must never intentionally obfuscate or hide costs.
* Cost tracking must be transparent and granular down to the individual Task.
* Budgets and quotas must be strictly configurable.
* The system must alert before limits are reached.
* Cost reports must be reproducible and auditable.

---

## Never Do

* **Never** allow a Task to execute if the Department has exceeded its hard quota, unless explicitly overridden by a Global Admin.
* **Never** hardcode pricing matrices into the core codebase; pricing changes constantly and must be fetched from a dynamic configuration registry.
* **Never** assume an external AI provider will enforce budgets for you; the Cost Engine must enforce limits locally to protect against runaway loops.
* **Never** delete historical cost records when a Workspace is archived; financial history is immutable.

---

## Cost Management Constitution
The permanent Cost Management principles of AegisAI:
1. **The Principle of Transparency**: If it costs money, it must be measured. If it is measured, it must be visible to the person paying for it.
2. **The Principle of Hard Limits**: An enterprise system cannot rely on hope. A budget is not a suggestion; it is a physical barrier that the AI cannot cross.
3. **The Principle of Attribution**: There is no such thing as an anonymous expense. Every fraction of a cent is tied to a Human, an Agent, and a Purpose.
